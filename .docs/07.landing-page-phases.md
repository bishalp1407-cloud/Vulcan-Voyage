## Narrative the page must deliver

```text
WHAT WE ARE
  → HOW WE THINK
  → WHAT WE BRING
  → HOW WE WORK
  → WHY WE ARE DIFFERENT
  → START A CONVERSATION
```

Visitor questions, in order:

1. What is this?
2. What can they help me with?
3. Why are they different?
4. Can they actually solve my problem?
5. How do they work?
6. What do they believe in?
7. What do I do next?

Visual rhythm (do not make seven identical sections):

```text
CALM → STATEMENT → INFORMATION → VISUAL INTERRUPTION
     → SYSTEM → PROCESS → MANIFESTO → DECISION
```

## Phase 0 — Lock the brief and content

**Objective:** Freeze the story, section list, and what will *not* ship, so build does not reopen strategy.

**Scope**

- Confirm the seven-section page flow and minimal nav.
- Write or lock section copy against the IA hierarchy (not marketing filler).
- Confirm contact actions: Call, WhatsApp, Email.
- Confirm pre-launch exclusions: no portfolio, testimonials, awards, service catalogue, full Design DNA, separate Vision/Mission/About.

**Deliverables**

- Approved section list and nav labels:
  - Thinking → Don’t Design the Obvious
  - Capabilities → What We Bring
  - Method → How We Work
  - Why VV
  - Bring Us a Problem
- First-pass copy for all seven sections, including hero statements:
  - Product Design Companion
  - Indian in origin. Lateral in thinking. Global in ambition.
  - DON’T DESIGN THE OBVIOUS.
- Contact details (or placeholders with a decision date).

**Out of scope:** Visual exploration, tech setup, motion.

**Done when:** Copy answers all seven visitor questions without extra sections.

## Phase 1 — Project foundation

**Objective:** Create a buildable system that already feels like VV, not a default app shell.

**Scope**

- Choose and record the tech stack in `04.techStack.md` (currently empty).
- Scaffold the app.
- Implement design tokens from the design system: colour, type scale, spacing, radius, motion durations.
- Implement layout primitives: `Container`, `Section`, grid, breakpoints.
- Set document defaults: warm canvas `#F5F3EE`, Inter/system sans as starting type, semantic HTML.

**Deliverables**

- Token file matching `.docs/05.designsystem.md` starter tokens.
- Empty page shell on the warm background with correct max-width (1440) and gutters.
- Filled `04.techStack.md`.

**Out of scope:** Finished sections, photography, accent colour (not locked).

**Done when:** A blank page uses VV colour, type, and spacing — not a generic white SaaS template.

## Phase 2 — Global chrome

**Objective:** Navigation and footer that stay minimal and always lead to the CTA.

**Scope**

- Desktop: logo, four jump links, persistent **Bring Us a Problem**.
- Mobile: logo + menu; menu includes the same links plus CTA.
- Footer: brand line, CTA, Call / WhatsApp / Email, copyright. No sitemap.
- Scroll behaviour: optional nav surface change; no login, no chat widget.

**Deliverables**

- `Navbar`, `Logo`, `Button` (primary + secondary), `Footer`.
- Smooth in-page anchors for Thinking, Capabilities, Method, Why VV, CTA.

**Out of scope:** Section interiors, motion beyond nav/CTA micro-interaction.

**Done when:** A visitor can jump to any major idea and always reach the conversion action.

## Phase 3 — Narrative skeleton (all seven sections, content-first)

**Objective:** Ship the full page as a readable, correctly ordered story before signature visuals.

**Scope**

Build all sections as structured content with `SectionLabel` numbering, not as card grids.

| # | Section | Visitor job | Visual role |
|---|---------|-------------|-------------|
| 01 | Hero | What is this and why keep reading? | Calm statement |
| 02 | What is Vulcan Voyage? | What can they help me with? | Structured information |
| 03 | Don’t Design the Obvious | Why are they different? | Visual interruption (placeholder ok) |
| 04 | What We Bring | Can they solve this? | System, not service cards |
| 05 | How We Work | How would they approach my problem? | One continuous process |
| 06 | Why VV | What do they stand for? | Manifesto |
| 07 | Bring Us a Problem | What do I do next? | Decisive close |

**Must include in this phase**

- Hero: brand, Product Design Companion, POV lines, supporting line, primary CTA.
- Definition of product design companion; audiences (start-up owners, business owners, product manufacturers); problem spaces; role from discovery to realisation.
- Core belief plus short lateral-thinking copy.
- Seven capabilities as one discipline set: Research, Lateral Thinking, Indian Context, Industrial Design, Engineering, Manufacturing, Brand. Arc: Need → Opportunity → Product → Reality.
- Nine method stages: Observe → Question → Reframe → Explore → Synthesise → Prototype → Test → Evolve → Realise.
- Why VV: Indian at the core, universal in relevance, built to endure, designed to evolve.
- CTA promise, invitation, button, Call / WhatsApp / Email.

**Out of scope:** Signature typography layouts, scroll-driven method, photography art direction, experimental motion.

**Done when:** The full story can be read top to bottom on desktop and mobile, with working CTA links.

## Phase 4 — Signature visual language

**Objective:** Make the page look like a product design studio thinking in public, not a brochure.

**Priority order (highest visual risk first)**

1. **Hero** — one strong gesture; statement, not banner. Observational product / material / diagram, not stock lifestyle.
2. **Don’t Design the Obvious** — signature section. Large type, high contrast, asymmetry, pause. This is the conceptual centrepiece.
3. **What We Bring** — capability matrix / connected field. Never seven service cards.
4. **How We Work** — one system (line, sequence, or timeline). Never nine isolated cards.
5. **What is VV** — editorial grouping of audiences and problem spaces.
6. **Why VV** — large statements, whitespace, manifesto blocks.
7. **CTA** — reduce visual intensity; simple and conclusive.

**Also in this phase**

- Image treatment: full-bleed, editorial crop, no default glass overlays.
- Technical artefacts only if they read as real design objects (dimension lines, diagrams, callouts).
- Cards only where containment is actually needed.
- Icons only for nav, contact, direction — not on every capability.

**Out of scope:** Heavy motion, typeface swap unless exploration is complete, locked accent colour unless it has a clear role.

**Done when:** Rhythm matches CALM → … → DECISION, and no section looks like a generic agency/SaaS block.

## Phase 5 — Motion and interaction

**Objective:** Motion that clarifies, reveals, or creates emotion — nothing else.

**Scope**

- Timing ~150–700ms, slow easing. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Allowed: type reveal, image enter, method stages connecting, nav change on scroll, CTA hover, diagram assemble.
- Forbidden: bounce, constant float, scroll hijack, cursor gimmicks, animation on every section, excessive parallax.

**Highest-value interactions**

- Method sequence revealing as one path from uncertainty to reality.
- Manifesto / Don’t Design the Obvious as a visual pause, not a loop.
- CTA as a calm conclusion.

**Done when:** Reduced-motion users get a complete page; motion users get fewer, stronger moments.

---

## Phase 6 — Responsive and accessibility

**Objective:** Mobile is a designed layout, not a squeezed desktop. Access is part of quality.

**Scope**

- Breakpoints: &lt;640, 640–1024, 1024–1440, &gt;1440.
- Preserve type hierarchy, stack complex layouts, keep strong imagery, keep some asymmetry.
- Keyboard nav, visible focus, labels, 44×44 touch targets, contrast, semantic headings.

**Done when:** Desktop, tablet, and mobile each feel considered; the CTA remains reachable without a mouse.

---

## Phase 7 — Polish, QA, launch

**Objective:** Ship a calm, credible pre-launch page that starts conversations.

**Scope**

- Copy pass: short, confident, no unverified claims.
- Real contact actions (or documented temporary placeholders).
- Performance: image weight, font loading, no decorative 3D/AI effects.
- Cross-browser check; broken-anchor check; CTA from hero, nav, section 07, and footer.
- Confirm exclusions still held (no fake portfolio/proof).

**Done when:** A start-up owner, business owner, or manufacturer can understand VV, feel the difference, and start a conversation in under one scroll-through.
